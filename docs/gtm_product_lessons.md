# GTM Product Lessons

## Buyer/user pain

Long business conversations contain useful signals, but the source material is slow to review and easy to interpret inconsistently. Users need help finding the evidence, not just reading another polished summary.

## Why generic summaries are not enough

A summary can compress the call while removing the audit trail. For product and GTM workflows, trust depends on showing the exact evidence span, source, reviewer decision, and evaluation history behind a signal.

## Why evidence/provenance matters

Evidence-backed outputs make the system easier to inspect, defend, and improve. Provenance also separates source acquisition from label creation, which prevents accidental mixing of weak suggestions with accepted truth.

## Human review is part of the product

Human review is not a weakness here. It is the governance layer that turns candidate signals into accepted labels. That review loop also creates the dataset needed to measure whether the system is getting better.

## Measurable quality improves trust

The repo tracks deterministic metrics, source-quality subsets, ML baseline behavior, and retrieval gates. This makes quality discussions concrete: what worked, what failed, what changed, and what needs more labels.

## Narrow workflows beat generic AI

The workflow is narrow by design: source, evidence, review, promotion, evaluation, report. That is more commercially useful than a broad assistant that cannot show why its output should be trusted.

## Land-and-expand implications

A practical entry point is one high-value review workflow with clear evidence and measurable quality. Expansion comes after users trust the review loop: more call types, more labels, better retrieval, and sharper stakeholder reports.

## Where this could become useful commercially

The pattern fits teams that review complex communication and need auditability: revenue operations, customer success, support QA, compliance review, product feedback analysis, and analyst/research workflows. This repo demonstrates the pattern; it does not claim each domain is fully implemented.
