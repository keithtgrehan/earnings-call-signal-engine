# Q&A Friction Spec

Q&A friction captures moments where analyst questions and management answers suggest pressure, unresolved concern, or evasive communication. It should be evidence-backed and conservative.

## Signal Types

- Analyst pressure: an analyst directly challenges assumptions, asks why investors should believe management, or presses on a negative trend.
- Repeated question: multiple turns revisit the same unresolved topic or ask for clarification after an incomplete answer.
- Evasive answer: management avoids the direct question, redirects to generic commentary, or answers a different topic.
- Hedging: management uses qualifiers such as "depends", "uncertain", "too early", "not ready to say", or "we will see" around a material topic.
- Pushback: management explicitly rejects, corrects, or resists the analyst framing.

## Evidence Requirements

Evidence should include the analyst question and, where possible, the management response. A single tense question is not enough if management answers clearly. A friction flag should identify the topic and why the exchange was unresolved or pressured.

## False-Positive Examples

- Routine clarification questions should not be labelled as pressure.
- Standard safe-harbor language should not be labelled as hedging.
- A confident but non-numeric answer should not be labelled evasive unless it avoids the asked topic.
- A polite analyst follow-up should not be treated as repeated pressure unless the same issue remains unanswered.

## Review Priority

Prioritize friction tied to guidance, demand, margins, cash flow, capex, churn, renewal, or major strategic commitments. Low-stakes wording issues should stay out of the benchmark until the core evaluation is stable.
